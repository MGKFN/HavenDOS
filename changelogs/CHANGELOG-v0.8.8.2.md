## 1. The text was stretched, and it was not the scaler

Reported as *"some parts unoptimized for the screen sizes and seems a
bit stretched"*, and before that as *"resolution works but is bugged and
small"*. It is real, it is in a screenshot, and it has been in every
framebuffer boot this system has ever done.

`drivers/vga.c` picks the glyph scale separately in each axis:

```c
sx = fb_width  / (cols * 8);
sy = fb_height / (rows * 8);
```

On a 1280x800 panel at 80x25 that is **sx=2, sy=4** — every character
drawn sixteen pixels wide and **thirty-two tall**. On 1920x1080 it is
3 and 5.

**The scale factors were not the mistake.** 80 columns and 25 rows of
8x8 cells is 640x200, an aspect ratio of **16:5**, and no panel is that
shape — so no pair of equal scale factors can fill one. The console had
a choice between leaving most of the screen black and stretching the
text, and it stretched the text.

80x25 of **8x16** cells is 640x400, which is **16:10**, and divides
1280x800 by exactly two in both axes. That is why every VGA text mode
in history used an 8x16 font for 80x25 and an 8x8 one only for 80x50.

### So "switch to real graphics" would have changed nothing

The console already *is* a framebuffer. `fbc_put()` walks the glyph bits
and writes pixels; there is no text hardware involved on these machines.
What was wrong was the shape of the cell those pixels made.

### Two changes, both needed

**`include/font8x16.h`** — a real 8x16 font. Generated by
`tools/make_font8x16.py` from `koi8x16.bdf`, in X11's
`font-misc-cyrillic`, whose licence is quoted in full in the header and
reads, in its entirety:

> koi12x24b.bdf, koi8x16b.bdf and koi8x16.bdf:
> May be distributed and modified without restrictions.

There is precedent: `include/font8x8.h` is Daniel Hepper's `font8x8`,
public domain, recorded the same way. A font was not invented here
because 256 glyphs drawn badly is worse than 256 drawn well, and the
part that actually needed deciding — the licence — has a written answer
rather than my opinion.

The accented range 0x80-0xFF is **composed**, not taken from that font:
koi8x16 puts Cyrillic there and HavenDOS puts CP437 accented letters
there. They are built from the 8x16 base letter plus an accent, using
the same table `tools/make_font_ext.py` uses for the 8x8 font, so the
two cell sizes draw the same shapes.

**One scale for both axes.** `fbc_fit()` now takes the *smaller* of the
two and centres what is left. A square cell is not negotiable and
filling the panel is, so on 1920x1080 at 80x25 the fit is scale 2 —
1280x800 of the panel used, centred, with bars — rather than characters
half again as tall as they are wide. `resolution fit` is the answer for
somebody who wants the whole panel: it picks a *grid* that fills it.

### What changed on screen

| panel | before | now |
|---|---|---|
| 1280x800, 80x25 | 16x32 cells, **2:1 stretch**, fills panel | 16x32 cells, **square**, fills panel |
| 1920x1080, 80x25 | 24x40 cells, **1.67:1 stretch** | 16x32 cells, square, centred |
| 1920x1080, `fit` | 120x67 at 8x8 doubled | 120x33, 16x32 cells |

**The text console is untouched.** Its 80x50 mode uploads an 8-row font
into plane 2 of the character generator and that hardware wants exactly
8 rows; `font8x8.h` still goes there, and `drivers/mode13.c` keeps its
own copy for 320x200.

### Three consequences worth knowing

- **240x135 no longer fits a 1080p panel.** 135 rows of a 16-pixel cell
  is 2160 scanlines. It was the headline grid of beta 1 and it was only
  ever expressible by distorting the glyph. `resolution` refuses it by
  name now.
- **2560x1440 is no longer "capped".** At 320x90 it is inside both axis
  limits and inside the cell budget, which it was not when a row was 8
  pixels.
- **`resolution fit` on 1280x800 gives exactly 80x25**, because that is
  precisely what the panel wants.

## 2. The desktop icon grid was two columns at any width

`shell/desktop.c` had the literal 2 in five places — `i/2` and `i%2` in
`draw_icons()`, in `icon_hit()`, in the context-menu anchor, and in the
arrow-key moves. Two columns of a 17-cell pitch is 34 cells, so on an
80-column console the right 45 columns were empty and on a 120-column
one it was 85.

The chrome around it already scaled: `TASKBAR_Y`, `INFO_Y` and
`ICON_ROWS_VIS` all come from `vga_rows()` and `vga_cols()`. The icon
field did not.

One function, `icon_cols()`, and everything derives from it — because
`icon_hit()` and `draw_icons()` disagreeing about where an icon is means
clicking one and launching another.

## 3. The 32 GB stick: the updater read FAT32 only

*"USB discovery for FAT32 32GB drives still broken AF for updating."*
Four releases running, and every time it was this:

`fs/update_src.c`'s USB source called `fat32_scan_volumes()` and nothing
else, and `update` printed, in small print, **"exFAT and FAT16 are not
read here"**. A USB stick of 32 GB or more is formatted **exFAT** by
default. The refusal was by design and correct and it was still the
wrong answer.

`fs/exfat.c` arrived in beta 2 and is **read-only**, which is exactly
and only what an update *source* needs — the writing goes to `/boot` on
the internal disk through `fs/fat16.c`, and nothing touches the stick.
So the driver that cannot be trusted to install can be trusted with
this.

FAT32 is still tried **first**, so a stick that worked before takes the
path it always took.

### Why exFAT and still not FAT16

Not an oversight, and the reason is not about the filesystem. A FAT16
source would hold `fs/fat16.c`'s mount open while `fs/update.c` writes
the system volume *through that same driver* — so the backup at step 3
would be looked for on the stick. `fs/exfat.c` is its own driver with
its own mount slot, exactly like `fs/fat32.c`, so holding it open costs
`fat16` nothing.

### And the message says which one failed

"No FAT32 volume" and "an exFAT volume that would not mount" send you to
two different places, so they are counted separately and named
separately. v0.8.6.5's lesson was that *"it only found the ESP"* was
four failures wearing one face.

## 4. A live bug in the primality test

`net/rsa.c` — Miller-Rabin and RSA key generation — arrived in the tree
during this session. **I did not write it.** I checked it instead of
duplicating it, and found this:

```c
    if(rounds == 0) rounds = 1;
    for(r = 0; r < 1; r++){        /* should be r < rounds */
```

**`rsa_is_probable_prime(n, 64)` was a one-round test.** Measured on
2284453 = 1069 × 2137, a composite built to have many strong liars: 37
of 200 runs called it prime at one round, and **29 of 200 at
sixty-four**. The extra witnesses were never drawn.

Key *generation* was never affected — `rsa_keygen_step()` has its own
witness loop and honours `rounds_needed` — so keys it produced are
sound. What was wrong is the function anything else would call to check
one.

### Why nothing caught it, which is the more useful half

`tests/test_rsa.c` passed **295 of 295**, and
`tools/check_rsa_suite.py` reported **10 of 18 planted defects
undetected** — including "the witness is always 2", "an even modulus is
accepted so 4 is probably prime", and "d is computed against phi
instead of lambda".

All ten lived in code the suite never ran. `rsa_is_probable_prime()`
proves primality below 997×997 by **trial division alone** — a composite
under that bound must have a factor at or below 997 and the table holds
every one of them — and **every Carmichael number and pseudoprime in the
vectors was below it.** 561, 2047, 3277, 4681: all caught by a division.
Miller-Rabin was unreachable from the suite.

That is the fixture being looser than the device, the same defect shape
as `make_exfat_fixture.py`'s first run — three contiguous files and no
fragmented one, so half the driver never executed.

`tools/make_rsa_vectors.py` now **constructs** hard cases instead of
listing them: Carmichael numbers from Chernick's formula with every
factor above 997, and strong pseudoprimes to bases 2 and 3 built as
p(2p−1) with both factors above it. Each is asserted to be composite and
to survive trial division before it is emitted. The check that compares
one round against sixty-four is what found the bug.

**17 of 18 injections are caught now**, then 18 of 18 after one more
check. The remaining gap — the top-two-bits forcing — needed a modulus
size that is not a multiple of 64 to reach the branch at all (530 bits
does it) *and* needed every candidate inspected rather than the finished
key, because with one bit unforced a key still looks right a quarter of
the time.

### Two of my own checks were wrong, and both are recorded

- A strong pseudoprime to base 2 **passes** a Miller-Rabin round with
  witness 2 — that is what the words mean. Pinning the entropy source
  at 2 and demanding "composite" is asserting something false. It
  passed on the small vectors only because trial division refused them
  first.
- A Carmichael number survives a *single* random witness about a third
  of the time, so a one-round group demanding "composite" passes or
  fails with the PRNG. It passed once and then stopped.

### And the suite went from nine minutes to ten seconds

The `p == q` test arranges a collision by pointing the entropy source at
the prime the generator just found, so every candidate is that prime —
which means every one survives trial division and all 64 witnesses
before being rejected, 20000 times. That is 1.28 million 256-bit modular
exponentiations, and it put `check_rsa_suite.py` past three hours, where
it was killed before finishing. **A harness nobody can run to the end
has proved nothing.** One witness per candidate in that block, and the
round count is asserted directly where it belongs.

## What is NOT verified

- **The mouse fix still has not run on the HP.** The boot log now
  reports no PS/2 mouse, which is correct and is what Kyan saw. Whether
  a **USB** mouse moves the cursor is the actual test of the beta-2 fix
  and is still unanswered.
- **The trackpad will not work and is not going to.** It is not behind
  the 8042; it is an I2C HID device and this kernel has no I2C driver.
  That is a release of its own, not a bug.
- **exFAT has still never read a real stick**, and the updater's exFAT
  arm has never read one either. It reads an image `mkfs.exfat` wrote,
  the FUSE driver populated and `fsck.exfat` called clean, and the suite
  demands the kernel it reads off exFAT is byte-for-byte the one it
  reads off FAT32.
- **The 8x16 font has only been seen in QEMU**, at 1280x800. The
  arithmetic is checked at fourteen panel sizes in `test_console`, but
  nobody has looked at a 1920x1080 laptop.
- **No TLS handshake has been made since `rsa_public_encrypt()` moved
  onto `net/bignum.c`** in beta 2.

## Still open

- **NTFS read-only** is a release; **ext4** is probably two. Requested,
  not started.
- **Stage 3 from the certificates on**: a DER encoder, X.509 v3, PKCS#1
  signing, a plist writer, and PEM→DER base64 (which makes three base64
  decoders in the tree and is the release where they become one). The
  bignum, Miller-Rabin and keygen are done. **A phone sharing its
  connection is not promised by any of this.**
- **What the "bugged" part of `resolution` looked like.** Asked three
  times, unanswered. The stretch and the grid persistence may or may not
  be what was meant.
- **The keygen time has still not been measured** on this kernel. The
  15-25 minute figure in `HANDOFF.md` came from the byte-wise
  `obn_powmod` and is wrong in the good direction. Measure it rather
  than quoting either number.
